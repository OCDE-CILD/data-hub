The Relationship Between Student Satisfaction with Environments and Academic Outcomes

The relationship between where a student lives and their outcomes is the strongest

By Research, Evaluation & Data Inquiry at OCDE Published June, 2026

Executive Summary

Orange County students feel satisfied about their environments outside school but are noticeably less satisfied with school itself. When looking at the relationship between satisfaction with different environments and academic outcomes, satisfaction with family and neighborhood are more closely related to academic and attendance outcomes than satisfaction with school. School experience was still positively related to outcomes but had the weakest relationship of the three. Ultimately, student success depends on more than what happens in the classroom.

Implications

  • School experience is the clearest opportunity. Lower satisfaction points to room to strengthen belonging, engagement, and support during the school day.
  • Collaboration is essential. Schools should engage families, partner with community organizations, and align supports across settings.
  • Student, family, and community voice should inform decisions.

Full Report

This year’s State of the Student report, produced by REDI, highlights both encouraging progress and important areas where continued focus is needed. The spotlight examines how students’ own experiences relate to key academic and engagement outcomes.

At the student level, data from the California Healthy Kids Survey show that students generally feel positive about important aspects of their lives outside of school. The majority reported being satisfied with where they live (81%), their friendships (75%), and their family life (77%). In contrast, students are less positive about their school experience, with only 46% reporting that they are satisfied or very satisfied. Notably, about one in four students (27%) report that they are not satisfied with their school experience. These comparatively lower ratings, along with a smaller share of students reporting being “very satisfied,” point to an opportunity for schools to strengthen students’ sense of belonging, engagement, and support during the school day.

Looking beyond individual responses, the school-level data reveal a clear pattern: students’ experiences outside of school are strongly connected to how well they do in school. Using a school-level correlation analysis of 146 schools, results showed that schools where students report higher levels of satisfaction with their family life and where they live tend to have stronger academic outcomes and better attendance. For example, satisfaction with where students live is strongly correlated with higher rates of students meeting or exceeding standards in Math (r = 0.742) and English Language Arts (r = 0.728), as well as lower rates of chronic absenteeism (r = -0.512). Similarly, satisfaction with family life is closely linked to stronger academic performance (Math r = 0.616; ELA r = 0.601) and fewer students missing school regularly (r = -0.598). These were among the strongest relationships observed in the data. However, satisfaction with school experience, although still positively correlated with academic outcomes, had the weakest relationship (Math r=0.411; ELA r=0.405).

Although these findings may not be surprising, they reinforce an important message: student success is shaped by more than what happens inside the classroom. Positive school experiences matter, but the conditions in students’ homes and communities also play a critical role in supporting learning, engagement, and well-being. These patterns indicate a need for strong, ongoing collaboration between schools, families, and communities. Improving student outcomes cannot rely on in-school efforts alone; it requires a more connected approach that reflects the full context of students’ lives. Schools that actively engage families, partner with community organizations, and align supports across settings are better positioned to meet both academic and non-academic needs.

Importantly, progress has not been experienced equally by all students, and disparities remain. This underscores the importance of elevating student, family, and community voices in decision-making. Students are telling us that their experiences differ across various aspects of their lives. By listening to and acting on that feedback, schools can create more responsive and inclusive environments that build on students’ strengths while addressing areas where they feel less supported. This also makes collaboration even more critical, particularly for students with higher and ongoing needs, whose challenges persist over time and require coordinated, sustained support.

Orange County’s progress is real and worth celebrating. Ensuring that every student shares in that success, however, will depend on how well communities and schools work together. By strengthening connections across school, home, and community, and by centering the voices of those most impacted, we can build on this progress and create more equitable outcomes for all students.
